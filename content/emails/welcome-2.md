The fastest way to know how a rug was made is to turn it over.

On a hand-knotted rug the pattern is just as sharp on the back as on the front, and if you look closely you can see each knot as a tiny square. On a machine-made rug the back is usually blurry, with a regular grid, or there's a glued backing hiding it altogether.

That's the whole test, and it's why every rug on the site has a video of its back.

Two honest limits, because I'd rather you hear them from me:

1. The flip test tells you **how** a rug was made. It doesn't tell you how old it is, where it came from, or what the fiber is. Those take other checks, and I show each one separately on the page.
2. Knots per square inch is a count, not a grade. A village rug at 40 knots can be heavier and more beautiful than a workshop rug at 100. The number tells you fineness and labor, not which one belongs in your home.
