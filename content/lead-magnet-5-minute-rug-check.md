## The checklist (one page, Chris's voice)

**Why this matters.** A hand-knotted 8x10 is a four-figure purchase. The rug-shop script counts on you not being able to check anything. These six checks take five minutes and you can do them from a listing, in a shop, or on a video call.

**Check 1: Flip it.** On a hand-knotted rug the pattern is just as sharp on the back, and you can see each knot as a small square. A tufted rug hides a glued cloth backing. A machine-made rug shows a blurry grid or a perfectly regular one. If a seller won't show you the back, that is your answer.

**Check 2: Read the fringe.** On a hand-knotted rug the fringe is the warp, the same threads that run through the rug. Follow one into the body of the rug. A fringe that is sewn or glued on means the rug was not knotted onto those threads.

**Check 3: Count the knots.** Lay a ruler on the back. Count the knots across one inch, then down one inch, and multiply. The number tells you fineness and how much work went in. It is a count, not a grade: a village rug at 40 knots can be heavier and more beautiful than a workshop rug at 100. What matters is that the seller will let you count.

**Check 4: Question the fiber.** Wool feels springy and slightly oily; it warms in your hand. Real silk is cool, heavy and lustrous from every angle; "art silk" (viscose or mercerized cotton) has a flat shine and crushes easily. Do not take a "silk" claim on trust, and do not run a burn test on a rug you have not bought. Ask for the fiber to be written on the invoice, or ask a professional to test it.

**Check 5: Question the certificate.** A certificate should name the specific rug (an ID, the exact size, the fiber, the region), who issued it, and how to check it. Ask: who wrote this, and can I contact them? A certificate that could describe any rug describes none.

**Check 6: Question the price story.** "Was $5,000, today $1,400" is a story about the seller, not the rug. Ask for one price and whether it includes shipping and duties. A price that moves when you stand up was never the price.

## What this does not tell you (honesty box)

- These checks tell you how a rug was made, not how old it is or exactly where. Origin needs a certificate you can verify and a seller who will answer questions.
- Knot count does not tell you which rug belongs in your home.
- A fiber check by hand is a screen, not a lab result. When the price depends on the fiber, get it in writing.
- Dyes cannot be judged from a photo. Ask whether they are natural, synthetic or mixed, and expect an honest "mixed" more often than "all natural".
